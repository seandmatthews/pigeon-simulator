# District theming: four dressed quadrants, one signature each — reference research

Concept slug: `district-theming`. Context: T-136 — each of the four city
quadrants stops being a palette swap and reads as a PLACE: main street
(marquee), tailgate lot (water tower), campus (campanile), residential
(grain silo), with one signature silhouette per quadrant restyled onto the
T-091 anchor posts (FORK D-A) or built as separate landmarks (FORK D-B).
This note records what the four real-world counterparts actually look like,
their real dimensions, which parts face which way, and photo-grounded
proposals for the ticket's dressing levers (tints, prop mixes, tree
densities, signature dimensions). Tone rule context: invented Ames-flavored
names only — the photos document structure and color, never marks.

## What it is

Four American small-city district archetypes (the "Ames, lightly
fictionalized" register from DESIGN.md), each with one signature structure
that reads from the air:

1. **Main street + movie-marquee theater** — a continuous two-row canyon of
   2–3 story brick shopfronts (`main-street-naas-aerial.jpg`: buildings wall
   BOTH sides of the street, rear alleys split the rows, cars park
   nose-to-curb; `geneva-marquee.jpg`: warm-brick facade, dark-green
   awnings, cream trim) with the theater's marquee projecting over the
   sidewalk at the entrance.
2. **Tailgate lot + municipal water tower** — the pregame edge: open lots
   of parked vehicles in rows (the already-banked `docs/references/
   tailgate-fans/` set holds the lot photos: `browns-muni-lot.jpg` asphalt
   rows, `meadowlands-aerial.jpg` aerial), on the edge of town, with the
   town's water tower rising over it — the one structure that carries the
   town's name in large letters on its tank.
3. **Campus quad + campanile** — a college green: a big open lawn dotted
   with mature trees, the campanile standing alone in it
   (`isu-campanile-full.jpg`: tower centered in mown lawn, tree clusters
   framing the edges) — Iowa State's campanile is the literal Ames
   original.
4. **Residential edge + grain silos** — quiet streets buried in tree canopy
   (`random-lake-tower-aerial.jpg`: houses nearly invisible under mature
   trees), and at the town's edge the farmstead marker: 2–4 silos beside a
   barn (`dane-county-silos.jpg`). Real silos never stand mid-block — they
   mark the field edge.

## Real dimensions and proportions

Fetched sources: Wikipedia articles on the [ISU Campanile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campanile_(Iowa_State_University)),
[water tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_tower), [silo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silo)
and [marquee (structure)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquee_(structure)) (all fetched
2026-10-24); the rest is read off the kept photos and labeled as such.

- **Marquee:** a structure placed over the entrance to a theater (etc.),
  projecting over the main entrance; its border is "a surrounding compound
  of light bulbs, usually yellow or white, that flash intermittently or as
  chasing lights" (fetched). 20th-century marquees grew trapezoid-shaped
  for readability from passing cars (fetched). No standard dimensions are
  published; from `geneva-marquee.jpg` proportions (against the door and
  windows below it): the sign box reads ≈ one storefront-story tall
  (≈ 3–4 m), ≈ one bay wide (≈ 6–8 m), projecting perpendicular off the
  facade ≈ 2–3 m, with soffit just above door-top height. The vertical
  blade above it (Geneva) rises one more story, edge-lit with bulbs.
- **Water tower:** "a standard water tower typically has a height of
  approximately 40 m (130 ft)"; the tank/reservoir is at minimum
  ≈ 6 m tall × 4 m diameter; the town name is painted in large letters on
  the tank as an aid for aviators and motorists (all fetched). The
  photographed Des Moines Allen Hazen tower: 110 ft (34 m), built
  1930–31, 1.7 million US gallons, 15 concrete Tuscan columns around a
  central pipe column, NRHP-listed — and it once carried a large painted
  arrow on top guiding pilots to the Des Moines airport (fetched; the
  photos match: column ring, entablature, drum tank with top railing,
  central riser). From `random-lake-tower-aerial.jpg` (modern single-column
  spheroid, the "golf ball on a tee" type per the fetched article): the
  tank bulges ≈ 3× the support-column diameter and the tank holds ≈ ⅓ of
  the total height.
- **Campanile (the Ames original):** Iowa State's is 110 ft (34 m) tall on
  a 16 × 16 ft (5 × 5 m) base — ≈ 7:1 height:width — built 1897–98 of
  brick and terra cotta, holding a 50-bell carillon (10 in 1899, grown to
  50 by 1967; heaviest bell 5,737 lb), with clock faces and bell-chamber
  arches (fetched). From both ISU photos: a slender square shaft rises
  plain for ≈ 75–80% of its height, the top tier is an arched OPEN belfry
  with the bells visible inside (`isu-campanile-angle.jpg`), a pyramidal
  slate spire caps it with corner pinnacles at the belfry, and a clock
  face sits on EACH flat face just below the belfry (visible on two faces
  across the two photos).
- **Grain silo:** tower silos run "typically 10 to 90 ft (3 to 27 m) in
  diameter and 30 to 275 ft (10 to 90 m) in height"; materials are wood
  staves, concrete staves, cast concrete and steel panels, including "the
  iconic blue Harvestore low-oxygen silos" (all fetched — the Dane County
  photo shows exactly one concrete-stave silo (gray, vertical stave seams,
  dome top) beside two blue Harvestore-style steel silos). From
  `dane-county-silos.jpg`: silos top out ≈ 1.5–2× the barn's roof, and
  farmsteads cluster 2–4 of them directly beside the barn, never
  freestanding in a field.

## Orientation of the parts (explicit)

- **Marquee:** the box projects PERPENDICULAR to the facade, hanging OVER
  the sidewalk/entrance. The letter board faces ALONG the street (readable
  walking toward the theater); the bulb strip runs around the box's outer
  PERIMETER (front + sides + soffit edge); the blade rises VERTICALLY from
  the marquee's street-facing edge. Julesburg variant: a horizontal blade
  sign projects off the wall face itself, reading ACROSS the facade, no
  letter board, no canopy.
- **Water tower:** tank sits ON TOP of the supports; the supports meet the
  ground at the tower's small footprint; the riser pipe runs through the
  CENTER of the structure (clearly visible in the Des Moines photo as a
  fat central column inside the column ring); the name band runs
  HORIZONTALLY around the tank's side, facing the town approach and
  readable from the air; antennas/ladders rise from the tank's top center.
- **Campanile:** square tapered shaft on a small base; a clock face on
  EACH of the four flat faces just below the belfry (never one side only);
  arched belfry openings pierce the top tier on all four faces; bells hang
  INSIDE the belfry, visible through the arches; the pyramidal spire sits
  ON the belfry tier, corner pinnacles at its four corners.
- **Silo:** a vertical cylinder with a dome top; the concrete-stave type
  shows vertical stave seams and horizontal hoop bands; the steel type is
  a smooth colored drum with a small cap vent. Silos stand BESIDE the
  barn's gable end, clustered (the photo shows 3), always taller than
  every barn roof; the cluster sits on the farmyard's field side.

## Common variants

- Marquee: full canopy box + letter board + vertical blade (Geneva) vs
  bare horizontal blade sign (Julesburg) vs neon-tube front (not
  photographed — no photo here shows a neon-tube marquee).
- Water tower: neoclassical column ring (Des Moines) vs single-column
  spheroid (Random Lake) vs the 4-leg splayed-steel-lattice type (common
  in small towns but NOT in this set — if the restyle wants the splayed-
  leg read, that leg runs on the tailgate-fans aerials' lot context, not
  here).
- Campanile: ISU's square Gothic brick shaft is the Ames register; Venice's
  St Mark's campanile turned up in the same search (wrong continent, not
  kept).
- Silo: concrete stave (gray, hooped) vs blue glass-fused steel
  (Harvestore) vs cast concrete; town-scale groups of them are grain
  elevators (per the fetched article), a bigger subject than this ticket.

## Signature restyle dimensions under FORK D-A (the anchor-post envelope)

The T-091 post envelope is fixed: plinth 1.4 × 1.0 × 1.4, shaft
0.7 × 13.6 × 0.7, head 1.8 × 2.2 × 1.8, cap 2.0 × 0.5 × 2.0 — total top
17.3 m above the slab. Photo-grounded mapping, strongest fit first:

- **Campanile (best fit):** shaft → plain tower shaft; head → arched
  belfry tier; cap → pyramidal spire. Real ISU ratio 7:1 (34 m on 5 m);
  the post runs 13.6 m on 0.7 m — slimmer, but the belfry-at-top-with-
  spire read is native to the tier stack. Real belfry share ≈ top 20%;
  the head+cap hold the top 2.7/17.3 ≈ 16% — close.
- **Water tower (good fit):** shaft → riser/column; head → the tank; cap →
  tank roof. The head tier is already 2.6× the shaft width — the Random
  Lake spheroid runs ≈ 3× (photo-measured), the Des Moines tank ≈ 2.5–3×
  its column ring — the tier is proportionally right as-is. Tank share of
  height: real ≈ ⅓; the head holds 2.2/17.3 ≈ 13% — the restyle could
  WIDEN the head mesh (e.g. toward the cap's 2.0 m) to read tankier; that
  changes only the anchor scenario's own constants, no placement math
  (nothing stands within 9.75 m horizontally, T-091's probe).
- **Silo (weakest fit):** the silo read is WIDTH — real silos are 3–27 m
  wide cylinders. A 0.7 m shaft reads as a pole. Proposal: widen the
  shaft MESH to the plinth's 1.4 m footprint (ground footprint unchanged →
  the plinth-daylight re-derivation stays well-formed), giving a slender
  16 m cylinder at ≈ 11:1 — honest note: this is a stylized silhouette,
  NOT a real-silo proportion; add hoop bands + dome cap to carry the read.
- **Marquee (stylized):** the head/cap become the projecting marquee box
  with a bulb-strip tint and the shaft below it as the "blade" mass. No
  real counterpart to scale against at post size — the Geneva photo's
  proportions are the look target, the post is an emblem of it.

Under D-A every signature is an emblem at 17.3 m — real landmarks run
34 m (both the ISU campanile and the Des Moines tower, fetched) to 40 m
(standard tower, fetched). **FORK D-B** (separate structures) is what buys
true scale — a 40 m tower on the lot corner, a fat 6 m-wide silo — at the
cost of new placement/clearance math and a second pin storm (T-091's own
record). The ticket recommends D-A; the research adds: campanile and water
tower re-skin cleanly onto the tier stack, the silo is the one that
suffers (width), and if the owner wants the TRUE-scale read anywhere, D-B
for the tailgate water tower alone (the district where the real structure
is most famously the town's name-bearer) would be the highest-value
exception. Owner picks.

## Quadrant ↔ theme mapping (owner pick)

The ticket's proposed table (0 NW warm brick → main street, 1 NE sandy
cream → tailgate lot, 2 SW cool blue-gray → campus, 3 SE sage green →
residential) is internally coherent and the photos support each pairing:
the marquee needs a shopfront row (warm brick reads main-street), the

tailgate lot needs openness (NE's sandy cream + the visitor-class lot
edge), the residential reads canopy + silo-at-edge (sage green). No
photo-grounded argument forces any permutation — the whole table is a
taste pick; alternatives are pure swaps with no cost asymmetry. Owner
confirms at sign-off.

## What the game should copy vs simplify

Copy the four distance-readable skeletons and their deltas: marquee =
bulb-bordered projecting box + letter board; water tower = narrow support
+ fat tank + name band + everything-sits-ON-TOP; campanile = plain shaft,
clock on EVERY face, open arched belfry, spire; silo = cylinder + dome +
hoop bands, clustered, at the town edge. Copy the district CANOPY deltas
(main street ≈ treeless, campus = framed lawn, residential = buried in
trees) — that contrast is the cheapest dressing read from cruise height.
Simplify: lettering is banned or invented-only (the name band renders
nothing unless the forge proves legible props — per the ticket's "not
claimed" list), chase-light animation (static warm emissive), stave/brick
texture detail (flat tints + band lines at this art scale), and real
scale (D-A emblems at 17.3 m, flagged above).
