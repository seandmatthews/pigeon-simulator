# Ames Survey — Landmark & Location Dossier

Date-stamped: 2026-11-08.
Scope: verified facts for rebuilding the game's procedural city as real Ames, Iowa
(ISU campus + town), to scale, with the major landmarks.

**Method:** every headline number was verified against at least one solid source
(official/university page, engineering or press source, or Wikipedia with a
citation); two sources were preferred for the headline figures and are flagged
where only one was found. Dimensions are metric-first with the original
imperial shown once per number. Anything we could NOT pin to a source is
marked **UNVERIFIED** — treat those as placeholders, not facts.

**Legal tiers used below:**
- `public` — public landmark (NRHP-listed, city-owned, or plain geography)
- `university` — university-owned real thing, name usable descriptively
  ("the campus bell tower"); exact name is a university mark
- `business` — trademarked business name; model the building, rename the sign

## 1. Campus orientation (read this first)

- The main ISU campus sits **southwest of downtown Ames**. The commercial
  grid (Main Street / 5th Street, Clark–Duff Avenues) is 2.93 km **due east**
  of the Memorial Union, across the Union Pacific main line.
- **Central campus lies NORTH of Lincoln Way**; the street passes along the
  **south** side of it at the Campustown stretch (the Memorial
  Union's street address *is* 2229 Lincoln Way, and Campustown (Welch Ave
  bars/shops) sits just **south** of the street). Official ISU driving directions route
  visitors I-35 → US-30 west → **University Boulevard** north "traveling to
  Lincoln Way" — University Blvd is the EAST gate of Campustown
  (−93.6333), not a campus edge on the west/southwest.
  *(QA fix 2026-11-08: orientation block corrected per QA-REPORT item 3 /
  A-01+A-03 — verification ledger V-01/V-03/V-04.)*
- The **UP (ex-C&NW) Overland Route main line** runs east–west between
  campus and downtown — the historic divide the railroad town was cut by.
- Sports campus (Jack Trice Stadium, Hilton Coliseum, Iowa State Center
  lots) occupies the **south/southwest corner**, along University Blvd.
- The big east–west streets for the game: **Lincoln Way** (old Lincoln
  Highway, dead E–W at bearing 269.3° through town), **US-30 four-lane** along the
  south edge, **13th St/Grand Ave** loops in the north. *(QA fix 2026-11-08:
  "diagonal-ish NE–SW" corrected per QA-REPORT item 3 / A-02 — ledger V-03.)*
- Reiman Gardens anchors the **south entrance** of campus on University Blvd.

Sources: ISU Career Services directions via University Blvd/Lincoln Way;
MU address (Wikipedia, mu.iastate.edu); Inside Iowa State construction
notices ("University Boulevard, south of Jack Trice Stadium").

### C1 — Campanile (Stanton Memorial Carillon)
Built 1897 (designed by George E. Hallett, built by J.F. Atkinson & Bro.,
$6,510.20, 50,000+ bricks), stands **110 ft (33.5 m)** on a 16×16 ft base.
**50 bells** (largest ≈ 2.6 t / 5,737 lb): Edgar W. Stanton (ISU class of
1872) donated the original 10-bell chime in 1899 in memory of his wife
Margaret MacDonald Stanton; 13 treble bells added 1956 and more in 1967
brought it to 50. Function: clocks on four faces, Westminster Quarters every
quarter hour, student carillon recitals. Legend/tradition: "campaniling" —
you become a true Iowa Stater by kissing someone under the Campanile at
midnight while the bells play. Model the open belfry arches; the brick
shaft is slender, not massive.
Sources: Wikipedia (Campanile (Iowa State University));
https://carillon.music.iastate.edu/history

### C2 — Marston Water Tower
**168 ft (51.2 m)** tall, erected **1897**; the tank alone is 40 ft
(12.2 m) tall and 24 ft (7.3 m) in diameter, capacity 162,000 US gal
(613 m³). Designed by Anson Marston, ISU's first dean of engineering, for
the campus water system. The claim **"first elevated steel water tank west
of the Mississippi River"** is verified verbatim on ISU's official
landscape marker 30-01 and repeated in the NRHP nomination. One campus
paper gives 150 ft — the official 168 ft figure wins. Retired from
service; NRHP-listed. From the air it is a pale pin-cushion (white/pale-gray
painted steel — photos, F-03): a plain steel cylinder taller than any nearby
academic building. *(QA fix 2026-11-08: "dark pin-cushion" corrected per QA-REPORT
item 3 / A-09.)*
Sources: https://www.fpm.iastate.edu/maps/memorials/marker.asp?id=30-01;
NRHP nomination (NPGallery); Iowa State Daily (dissenting 150 ft figure noted).

### C3 — Beardshear Hall
Central Building completed **1906** (finish work through 1908; Proudfoot
& Bird, ~$400,000), renamed for President William Beardshear. ~**104,292
gross sq ft (9,689 m²)** per ISU Facilities Planning. Houses President,
Provost, Treasurer, Registrar. The **oxidized-copper green dome** over the
east entrance is the silhouette icon; campus legend says it's green
because it's the color of students' money. Long three-story pale-stone
block closing the north side of the lawn.
Sources: https://www.fpm.iastate.edu/maps/buildings/building.asp?id=13;
Wikipedia (Beardshear Hall); ISU Library historic exhibits.

### C4 — Parks Library
Original Bedford-limestone building **1925** (footprint 176×130 ft /
53.6×39.6 m, ~$400k), named W. Robert & Ellen Sorge Parks Library in
**1984**; centennial celebrated 2025. Today **325,488 gsf (30,238 m²)**
over five floors. The fourth-largest object on the quad after the
stadium/arena; a big limestone block with a stepped, planted roofline.
Sources: https://www.lib.iastate.edu/visit-and-study/art-in-library/architecture;
ISU Library 150-years exhibit.

### C5 — Memorial Union
Opened **September 23, 1928** as a living WWI memorial (designed 1922 by
William T. Proudfoot), address **2229 Lincoln Way** at the corner of
Lincoln Way & Morrill Rd/Lynn Ave — one block east of Stanton (QA fix
2026-11-08 per QA-REPORT item 3 / A-05; ledger V-08). Original five-story section + Great Hall and
Gold Star Hall, then ~11 additions (1938, 1948, 1950, 1957-58, 1964-65,
1972, 1978-79; north facade completed in three phases). Role: student
union, bookstore, bowling, wedding venue — the "front door" of campus.
**Tower caveat:** a south (Lincoln Way) tower is part of the building's
face; a circulating "~148 ft" height could NOT be verified against any
authoritative source — do not bake that number in. Verified headline is
the building mass and its corner presence.
Sources: Wikipedia (Memorial Union (Iowa State University));
https://www.mu.iastate.edu/architecture

### C6 — Jack Trice Stadium
Opened **Sept 20, 1975** as Cyclone Stadium (field = Jack Trice Field from
1975); **renamed Jack Trice Stadium in 1997** after student activism,
honoring ISU's first Black athlete (died of game injuries, 1923). Official
capacity **61,500** (since 2015; opened at 42,500). Surface: natural grass
since 1996; field sits slightly below surrounding grade; double-decked
sideline grandstands, enclosed south end zone, 3-level press/suite tower
(1997, $6.2M), north videoboard 36×79.5 ft (11×24.2 m) since 2011; north
end zone is a grass hillside (seating included in the 61,500). Surrounding
tailgate lots north and east (Olsen Building site became a plaza/tailgate
space); Stark Performance Center (2019-21, 100,000 sq ft) adjoins.
**Field orientation: NORTH–SOUTH — settled (ledger V-09; pitch bbox 53 m E–W ×
113 m N–S; the earlier "Wikipedia states east–west" line was a misquote —
Wikipedia makes no orientation claim).** Sits directly along University Blvd,
south campus. *(QA fix 2026-11-08 per QA-REPORT item 3 / A-06.)*
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Trice_Stadium;
cyclones.com facilities; Inside Iowa State.

### C7 — Hilton Coliseum
Opened **December 2, 1971** (ground broken 1968), part of the Iowa State
Center; named for president James H. Hilton; capacity **14,267**. Home of
Cyclone basketball and the "Hilton Magic" home-court legend. Replaced the
6,000-seat Armory. Shape from the air: a wide, low, many-sided concrete
drum with a shallow arc roof, surrounded by the Iowa State Center parking
sea — the roundest thing in town after the water towers.
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilton_Coliseum;
https://cyclones.com/news/2011/12/2/205341266

### C8 — Morrill Hall
Built **1891** — second-oldest surviving campus building (after Farm
House). Served as library, chapel, classrooms; reopened **March 2007**
after ~$7-9M restoration; **27,172 sq ft (2,524 m²)**; now houses the
Christian Petersen Art Museum (Petersen was the nation's first permanent
campus artist-in-residence). Red brick, tall round-arched windows.
Sources: https://museums.iastate.edu/christian-petersen-art-museum;
Wikipedia (Morrill Hall (Iowa State University)).

### C9 — Farm House (Knapp–Wilson House)
Built **1860–61** — the **oldest building on campus**, pre-dating
students/classes; first home of the Model Farm superintendents (later
deans "Cyum" Knapp and James Wilson lived there — hence Knapp–Wilson
House). Designated a **National Historic Landmark in 1966** (NRHP
#66000339); first bricks made on college grounds went into its walls.
Now a University Museums house museum. Two stories, red brick, white
trim, front porch, on a slight knoll east of Marston Hall.
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Farm_House_(Knapp%E2%80%93Wilson_House);
ISU Library historic exhibits.

### C10 — Reiman Gardens
**17 acres (6.9 ha)**, 26 garden areas (official site), at **1407
University Blvd** as the striking south entrance to campus/town. Key
structures: **Christina Reiman Butterfly Wing** — 2,500 sq ft (232 m²)
80°F tropical glasshouse, up to ~800 live butterflies (up to 80 species),
advertised as the nation's largest such exhibit; **Hughes Conservatory** —
5,000 sq ft (465 m²) glasshouse with quarterly seasonal displays.
**Entrance canopy caveat:** the entrance complex's butterfly-wing-shaped
canopy is real and well known on campus, but we found no sourced dimension
for it — model it as a large sculptural wing-form canopy over the entry
drive, scale to look, mark UNVERIFIED for exact spans.
Sources: https://www.reimangardens.com/garden-spaces; Discover Ames.

### C11 — Lake LaVerne
Constructed **1915–16** from a gift by alumnus LaVerne Noyes; Iowa DNR
lists the surface at **2.80 acres (11,330 m²)**. **Swan tradition:** a
pair of mute swans named **Sir Lancelot and Elaine** was presented during
**VEISHEA 1935** and the lake has kept a Lancelot-and-Elaine pair ever
since (at times two females; cygnets hatched 1944, 1970, 1971; Elaine
retired Nov 2022 pending the $5.1M restoration). A **$5.1M dredging
(2024–2028)** is taking max depth from 5.5 ft (1.7 m) to 12 ft (3.7 m) and
adding a new bridge. Verify the area claim: DNR's 2.80 ac is the number
(older folklore says "5 acres" — wrong). The pond sits between the MU and
Union Drive with the Carillon framing the postcard shot.
Sources: Iowa DNR lake data; ISU Lake LaVerne Improvements Project
(operationsfinance.iastate.edu); isuspecialcollections.wordpress.com
(Cardinal Tales, "Lancelot and Elaine").

### C12 — Central Campus
Planned by president A.S. Welch in **1869** as an open lawn encircled by
buildings, refined by the **Olmsted Brothers (1906 report)**. Core campus
~**490 acres (198 ha)** of trees and plantings; the dominant feature is a
**~20-acre (8.1 ha) lawn** bounded by the MU–MacKay axis (E–W) and the
Beardshear–Curtiss axis (N–S). Regularly ranked among the most beautiful
US campuses. For the game this is the biggest single open shape in town —
get its rectangle right and the campus reads instantly.
Sources: ISU Library "150 Years" exhibit
(https://digital.lib.iastate.edu/exhibits/150years/campus-buildings.html);
ISU land-grant legacy site.

### C13 — Lincoln Way/Union Drive underpass (and bridges)
The pedestrian underpass at **Lincoln Way & Union Drive** (sidewalk
approaches + pedestrian-only signal phase, 2021) is the classic safe
route from Campustown into the MU. The **East Gateway pedestrian bridge over
University Blvd to Jack Trice Stadium ("Cy Walk") has been OPEN since ~2024**
(span installed June 2022). *(QA fix 2026-11-08: the stale 2026 "building" line
corrected per QA-REPORT item 3 / A-08 — ledger V-17.)* Historic
named crossings in town: the **Grand Avenue rail crossing**, the
**College Creek Bridge** (Lincoln Highway era), and the **Skunk River
bridges** (see ameshistory.org transportation pages).
Sources: Inside Iowa State (2021 "Pedestrian-friendly"; 2026 campus
improvements); ameshistory.org Lincoln Way collection.

### T1 — Downtown Main Street Historic District
The **Ames Main Street Historic District** was listed on the **National
Register of Historic Places in May 2018**; boundaries per the City run
from the east side of Clark Avenue to the west side of Duff Avenue,
spanning the downtown commercial blocks around Main Street and 5th Street
(the UP main line cuts through this grid, historically between Main and
5th, with the old switching yards south of Main before they moved east).
Designated an Iowa **Cultural & Entertainment District**; on the Lincoln
Highway Heritage Byway. Contributing buildings include City Hall and the
YSS building; the restored **Orpheum Theater** is the marquee cinema.
Sources: Ames Tribune (May 21, 2018);
https://www.cityofames.org/My-Government/Departments/Planning/Historic-Preservation/National-Register-of-Historic-Places-Ames;
downtownames.org.

### T2 — Bandshell Park & the City Auditorium
Bandshell Park (125 E 5th St, **2.5 acres**, NRHP-listed) was accepted by
the city in **1884** as a gift from railroad interests; a concrete
bandstand was built 1909; the current bandshell was built **1934** and
refurbished 2005. The **Ames Municipal Band** (Cornet Band from 1910,
renamed 1924) plays free Thursday concerts every June–July. The Ames City
Auditorium (rentable hall) is at **515 Clark Ave**. From above: small
green square with a white shell, hard against the downtown grid.
Sources: https://ameshistory.org/content/ames-bandshell-and-park;
cityofames.org arts/culture pages.

### T3 — City Hall & Fire Stations
City Hall is at **515 Clark Ave** (same address block as the City
Auditorium) inside the historic district. **Fire Station 1 / HQ** opened
at **1300 Burnett Ave** in 1980 (dedicated Oct 26, 1980; department
organized as volunteers in 1895; five stations today). **Station 2 (132
Welch Ave, 1966)** sits in Campustown — useful for the game because it is
inside the student district. Sources: cityofames.org Fire history;
Firefighting wiki (station list); city directories.

### T4 — Fareway (Ames presence vs Boone origin)
Fareway is a **Boone, Iowa company**, not an Ames one: founded
**May 12, 1938** by Paul S. Beckwith, first store at 624 Story Street,
**Boone**; HQ stayed in Boone until a **2023 announcement moved it to
Johnston, IA**. The Ames hook: the Ames City Council approved a grocery
construction permit **January 17, 1938** — months before the Boone store
opened — so Ames is part of the origin story (Notes on Iowa). Today:
100+ stores in ~6-7 states; the Ames full-service store is **619 Burnett Ave**
(+ 3619 Stange Rd); **3720 Lincoln Way is a Fareway Meat Market (#189)**;
**534 S Duff Ave is the Walmart Supercenter** — the earlier "Ames store =
534 S Duff (store #792)" line was wrong, and #792 is unconfirmed. *(QA fix
2026-11-08 per QA-REPORT item 3 / A-07; ledger V-15.)* For the game: model a generic big-box grocery with a red-striped
sign; using the Fareway NAME on a sign is a trademark call — rename it.
Sources: https://www.fareway.com/about/history;
https://www.notesoniowa.com/post/fareway-s-origin-story-iowa-time-machine-january-17-1938.

### T5 — Ames Municipal Airport (KAMW)
Opened **August 1943**; ~283 ha (700 ac); field elevation 291.3 m
(955.6 ft) MSL; **no control tower**. Runways: **1/19 — 1,738×30 m
(5,701×100 ft) asphalt**; 13/31 — 1,064×23 m (3,491×75 ft) concrete
(right traffic on 13). Located ~3.2 km (2 mi) **southeast** of downtown.
The runway pair makes a distinctive off-axis cross — easy silhouette.
Sources: https://www.airnav.com/airport/kamw;
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ames_Municipal_Airport; cityofames.org.

### T6 — Union Pacific main line & yard — STATUS CORRECTED
The owner brief said "major UP hump yard — verify." **Verification
result: that claim does not hold up.** What the sources support:
- Ames was **laid out in December 1864 by the C&NW** (named for
  Congressman Oakes Ames by RR president John Blair) — a pure railroad
  town; the C&NW dominated for ~130 years until UP absorbed it in
  **April 1995**.
- The original **switching yard sat immediately south of Main Street**
  downtown; the 1902 plat caption says it was **moved east of Ames** —
  that east-of-town yard is where yard trackage lives today.
- The C&NW's famous humps were elsewhere (Proviso and Blue Island in
  Chicago; Clinton, Iowa's big yard; Boone's yard 15 mi west as the
  division point). Ames today is a **double-tracked Overland Route
  through-line with local/transfer switching (e.g., job Y-IT61)**, not a
  major classification or hump yard. North American hump yards are down
  from 152 (1975) to ~36; Ames is not on that list.
Game takeaway: model the main line as the town's spine (2 tracks,
90 mph-ish geometry, trains constantly rolling between Main/5th), with a
short yard/industrial lead east of downtown — don't build a hump.
Sources: https://ameshistory.org/content/chicago-northwestern-railroad
(incl. 1902 plat + 1960s aerial captions); Trains.com "North America's
hump yards" (Dec 2024); UP/C&NW merger history (April 1995).

### T7 — Grain elevators
The historic downtown elevator (Froning & Froning, 213 Duff Ave; ex-Ames
Grain Co.) was demolished via a fire-department training burn. Today the
visible elevator cluster is **southeast**, along the UP line toward
S Duff / SE 16th; **Todd & Sargent** (major grain-elevator construction
firm) is headquartered at 2905 SE 5th St. **Heights: no source found —
typical Iowa concrete silos run ~30-40 m (~100-130 ft); mark UNVERIFIED
and eyeball against imagery.** Sources:
https://ameshistory.org/tribunearchives/froning-froning-elevator;
ameshistory.org "Burning the Downtown Elevator"; USDA co-op directories.

### T8 — City water towers (besides Marston)
Ames runs **three elevated tanks, ~4 million US gallons (15,100 m³)
combined**. The named one: **BRET — Bloomington Road Elevated Tank**,
built **1990**, holds **2 million gal (7,570 m³)** and anchors north
Ames. A 1M-gal tower on **North Dakota Avenue** (near Ontario St) was
removed starting Aug 11, 2015. The city's new water treatment plant
(open-house Apr 19, 2025) is 15 MGD and serves ISU too. Generic
mushroom-bulb tanks; paint "AMES" on them.
Sources: https://www.cityofames.org/My-Government/Departments/Water-Pollution-Control/History-of-Ames-Water;
City of Ames Facebook post (BRET).

### T9 — I-35 and US-30 relative to town
**I-35 runs north–south on Ames's east side; US-30 is the four-lane
east–west bypass along the south edge**, and they meet at a full
interchange at the **southeast corner** of the city. US-30's grade-separated
interchanges across town: **Dayton Ave (northwest terminus of the bypass),
Grand Ave, Dakota/Elwood, Duff Ave (southeast)**. Active projects (2025-29):
I-35 bridge replacement over US-30, US-30 lowered for clearance, and the
2029 start of **six-laning I-35 through the US-30 interchange**. US-30
crosses the South Skunk River between Duff and Dayton.
Sources: Iowa DOT program notices via press summaries (KCCI, Jeff
Morrison's Iowa highway plan coverage); cityofames/Iowa DOT public-hearing
records (2015 US-30 corridor).

### T10 — Ada Hayden Heritage Park
~**190 acres (77 ha)** around **two former quarry-pit lakes**; opened
**July 2004** (city bought 437 acres via a 2001 bond), named for ISU
botanist Ada Hayden. Paved trails ≈ **4.5 km (2.8-3 mi)** — outside loop
1.9 km (1.2 mi), north loop 2.3 km (1.4 mi) — plus ~3.2 km (2 mi) of
crushed-rock paths; full circuit ~5-6.4 km (3.2-4 mi). Address 5205 Grand
Ave, north Ames. The twin lakes with pale quarry banks are the single
most identifiable shape in north Ames from the air.
Sources: https://www.cityofames.org/My-Government/Departments/Parks-and-Recreation/Parks/Ada-Hayden-Heritage-Park;
AllTrails/county trail data.

### T11 — Inis Grove Park
~**58 acres (23 ha)** at 2500 Duff Ave; core 40-acre tract donated by
Walter Grove in **1949** as a memorial. Amenities: two playgrounds —
including the **Ames Miracle Field & Barnes Family Inclusive Playground
(2020)** — trails, 4 tennis/pickleball courts. Wooded, creek-textured,
east side of town near the hospital district.
Sources: cityofames.org park listing; Discover Ames.

### T12 — Brookside Park
**83 acres (33.6 ha)** (city figure; one trail site says ~97 — use the
city's number) at **1325 6th St**, along **Ioway Creek** between downtown
and campus; founded **1925** (100th anniversary 2025). ~40 acres of older
gallery forest; shelters, wading/spray pool, ball fields, off-leash dog
area; Ioway Creek streambank stabilization project began June 2025.
From the air: the biggest dark tree mass inside the city grid.
Sources: cityofames.org parks; City of Ames "Brookside Park Celebrates
100 Years" bulletin; Bird Friendly Iowa.

### T13 — Ames History Museum (and Hoggatt School)
Ames History Museum at **416 Douglas Ave #101** (downtown; also runs the
**Hoggatt School**, an 1861 one-room schoolhouse, and hosts the "Jack
Trice 100" exhibit). Small storefront museum — minor from the air, but
its research archive is THE local source for anything else we model.
Source: https://ameshistory.org (site footer/contact).

## 4. History & culture quick facts

- **"Nation's first land-grant institution" — the precise claim:** Iowa
  Agricultural College was created by the Iowa legislature in **1858**
  (original 648-acre farm bought for $5,379). Iowa was **the first state
  to accept the Morrill Act's terms — September 11, 1862** — and the
  college received the land-grant designation in **1864**, which is why
  ISU styles itself "the nation's first land-grant university." Fine
  print people actually cite: **no acre of Story County was ever granted**
  — the Morrill Act gave states federal land *scrip* (30,000 acres per
  member of Congress) to sell to endow the college.
  Sources: https://www.landgrant.iastate.edu/morrill-act-1862; ISU Library
  150-years exhibit; Wikipedia (Iowa State University).
- **George Washington Carver at ISU:** first African American to enroll
  (**1891**), first Black graduate (BS **1894**), MS **1896**, and ISU's
  first Black faculty member, before leaving for Tuskegee in 1896. Iowa
  State celebrates an annual George Washington Carver Day.
  Source: https://www.cals.iastate.edu/news/2013/celebrating-150-years-george-washington-carvers-legacy.
- **The Ames Straw Poll (retired):** GOP presidential straw poll at ISU,
  held **1979–2011** (1979, 1987, 1995, 1999, 2007, 2011); last one
  **Aug 13, 2011** (won by Michele Bachmann); **canceled by the Republican
  Party of Iowa on June 12, 2015**. It filled the Hilton Coliseum and the
  Jack Trice tailgate lots with national media every cycle it ran.
  Sources: Politico (Jun 12, 2015); NYT (Jun 12, 2015).
- **VEISHEA** (the giant student festival whose 1935 parade gave Lake
  LaVerne its swans) was discontinued after 2014 — worth knowing so we
  don't build props referencing a dead tradition.
- **Cyclones identity:** the athletic nickname dates to September 1895
  press coverage ("Iowa will soon be famous for its cyclones") after a
  football upset; Cy the cardinal mascot; cardinal & gold everywhere —
  the town's dominant accent color for modeling.
- **Lincoln Highway:** Ames sits on the original Lincoln Highway; Lincoln
  Way preserves the name; the city is a stop on the Lincoln Highway
  Heritage Byway.
- **Jack Trice (1902-1923):** ISU's first Black athlete, killed by injuries
  in his second football game (Oct 1923); the 1997 stadium renaming and
  the famous "Toil when tired..." letter make him the moral center of
  campus iconography. Jack Trice Stadium is the only D-I FBS stadium
  named for a Black man — use with respect in any signage.
